## 1. B2B SaaS {#b2b-saas}

### Sub-types
- **SMB SaaS** (ACV <$5K): Self-serve, low-touch, PLG or inbound
- **Mid-market SaaS** (ACV $5K–$50K): Inside sales, content-led, some PLG
- **Enterprise SaaS** (ACV >$50K): Field sales, ABM, relationships

### GTM Model Selection
```
ACV < $2K/yr     → PLG (self-serve), SEO, product virality
ACV $2K–$15K/yr  → Inbound + inside sales, content marketing
ACV > $15K/yr    → Outbound + ABM, sales-led, events
```

### Acquisition Channels by Stage

**Pre-launch / 0 customers:**
- Cold outreach (email/LinkedIn) to 50 ideal prospects
- Founder's personal network → warm intros
- Niche communities (Slack groups, Discord, Reddit, forums)
- Write content where your ICP hangs out (not a company blog — targeted guest posts)

**Early traction (10–100 customers):**
- Double down on what got first customers
- Cold outbound with tight ICP definition
- Content: case studies with logo permission, LinkedIn founder content
- Partner with adjacent tools serving same ICP

**Growth (100+ customers):**
- Inbound content engine (SEO + thought leadership)
- Paid LinkedIn / Google Ads (after knowing CAC)
- Partner/integration marketplace
- Customer success → expansion → referral program

### ICP Signals for B2B SaaS
- **Trigger event:** Funding round, team growth, new tool adoption, regulation change, competitor switched
- **Pain indicators:** Job postings, tech stack (BuiltWith, LinkedIn), company growth rate
- **Best-fit profile:** Company recently scaled past the point where the current solution breaks

### Key Metrics
- CAC (by channel), LTV, LTV:CAC ratio (target >3x)
- Payback period (target <12 months for VC-backed, <6 months bootstrapped)
- NRR / NDR (target >100% for strong PLG, >120% for best-in-class)
- Time-to-value (first moment of value in product)

### Common Mistakes
- Targeting too broad an ICP ("all SMBs")
- Building features instead of fixing onboarding
- Ignoring churn while scaling acquisition
- Pricing too low and attracting wrong customers

## 2. B2C / Consumer App {#b2c}

### Sub-types
- **Freemium apps:** Revenue from % of free users upgrading
- **Subscription apps:** Direct recurring revenue
- **Ad-supported:** Revenue from attention, not payment

### GTM Model Selection
```
Viral product (sharing built-in)  → PLG + viral loops
Network effects                   → Community + referral program
Individual utility                → Content + paid acquisition + ASO
```

### Acquisition Channels by Stage

**Pre-launch:**
- Build an audience before the product (Twitter/X, TikTok, YouTube, newsletter)
- Waitlist with referral mechanism (viral loop)
- ProductHunt / Hacker News launch
- Niche community infiltration (Reddit, Discord)

**Early traction:**
- App Store Optimization (ASO) if mobile
- Influencer / creator partnerships (micro > macro for early stage)
- Content marketing targeting the job-to-be-done (YouTube SEO powerful here)
- Referral program: give value for sharing

**Growth:**
- Paid social (Meta, TikTok Ads) once CAC is known
- SEO / content at scale
- Platform partnerships (App Store features, integrations)
- Community-led growth (build a community around the use case, not the brand)

### Viral Mechanics
- **Inherent virality:** Product is better when shared (collaboration, social proof)
- **Incentivized referral:** "Give a month, get a month" (Dropbox model)
- **Content virality:** Output from the product is shareable (Canva designs, Spotify Wrapped)
- **Status virality:** Using the product signals something positive about the user

**Viral coefficient formula:** k = i × c (invitations sent × conversion rate). k > 1 = viral growth.

### Key Metrics
- DAU/MAU ratio (engagement quality, target >20% for strong apps)
- D1/D7/D30 retention (industry benchmarks: D1 >40%, D7 >20%, D30 >10%)
- CAC vs LTV (consumer apps: LTV often 3–5x CAC minimum)
- Virality coefficient (k-factor)

### Common Mistakes
- Paying for acquisition before retention is fixed
- Building for everyone, not a specific use case
- Underestimating ASO / organic channels
- Ignoring the social/emotional job (only optimizing functional job)

## 3. Marketplace {#marketplace}

### Core Challenge
Marketplaces have a **chicken-and-egg problem** — supply needs demand, demand needs supply.
The key strategic question: **which side is the bottleneck?**

### Solving the Chicken-and-Egg Problem

**Strategy 1: Constrained launch (geographic / niche)**
Launch in one city, one vertical, one use case. Dominate that micromarket before expanding.
> Example: Uber started in San Francisco only. Airbnb focused on conference overflow in NYC.

**Strategy 2: Fake the supply side**
Manually provide supply before it exists organically to attract demand.
> Example: OpenTable manually created restaurant listings. Reddit created fake accounts early on.

**Strategy 3: Lead with tools for one side**
Build value for supply side first (SaaS tools), then convert them to marketplace participants.
> Example: Shopify built e-commerce tools → then added marketplace discovery.

**Strategy 4: Single-player mode**
Make one side of the marketplace valuable even without the other side.
> Example: Figma is useful solo, becomes more valuable with teams.

### GTM Model by Marketplace Type

**Supply-constrained** (rare supply, abundant demand): Focus 80% effort on supply acquisition
- Examples: Luxury rental, skilled freelancers, specialized services
- Strategy: Concierge onboarding, direct outreach to supply, supply-side subsidies

**Demand-constrained** (abundant supply, hard to get demand): Focus 80% effort on demand acquisition
- Examples: Most B2B SaaS marketplaces, new verticals
- Strategy: Content marketing, SEO, paid acquisition, brand building

**Balanced:** Build both sides simultaneously with tight geographic / niche focus

### Key Metrics
- **GMV** (gross merchandise value) and **take rate** (% marketplace captures)
- **Liquidity:** % of listings that transact in a given period (target >10%)
- **Supply-demand ratio:** Are listings per category healthy?
- **Repeat rate:** Do buyers and sellers come back? (marketplace health indicator)

### Common Mistakes
- Launching nationally before proving one market
- Ignoring which side is the actual constraint
- Subsidizing the wrong side
- Building features instead of solving the liquidity problem

## 4. Professional Services / Consulting {#services}

### GTM Reality
Services businesses run on **trust + relationships**. Marketing plays a supporting role to sales.
Distribution = personal brand + referral network.

### Acquisition Channels by Stage

**0 → First clients:**
- Warm outreach: former colleagues, ex-clients, personal network
- LinkedIn content: demonstrate expertise publicly, not sell
- 1:1 coffee conversations: build relationships before pitching
- Strategic pro-bono: one showcase project with a reference-able name

**Growing beyond referrals:**
- Founder/principal thought leadership (LinkedIn, podcast appearances, speaking)
- Case studies: specific results, specific client (with permission)
- Strategic partnerships: adjacent services firms who cross-refer
- Niche down: be the expert for a specific type of company or problem

**Scaling:**
- "Productize" service into packages → reduces sales friction
- Content (articles, frameworks) that rank for problem-aware searches
- Community building around the problem space

### Positioning for Services
The most important positioning decision: **niche vs. generalist**.
- Generalist = more inbound volume, harder to win, price pressure
- Specialist = less inbound, higher win rate, premium pricing, more referrals

Rule: **The riches are in the niches**, especially early.

### Key Metrics
- Revenue per client, utilization rate (% of capacity billed)
- Referral rate (% of new clients from referrals — target >50% at steady state)
- Proposal win rate (target >40% for well-qualified pipeline)
- Average project duration / repeat project rate

## 5. Developer Tools {#dev-tools}

### GTM Reality
Developers are a unique audience:
- They hate being sold to
- They deeply trust peers and community signals
- They evaluate tools by trying them, not by reading marketing
- They are powerful internal champions if they love the tool

### GTM Model
Almost always starts **bottom-up / developer-led** even if selling to enterprises eventually.

### Acquisition Channels

**Pre-launch / early:**
- Hacker News "Show HN" launch (highest-quality early adopters)
- GitHub presence: open-source component or plugin, README with clear value prop
- Dev communities: specific Slack groups, Discord servers, Reddit (r/programming, niche subreddits)
- Founder active in communities before launch (be genuinely helpful, not promotional)

**Early traction:**
- Developer content: tutorials, how-tos, solve specific technical problems (YouTube + blog)
- Integration with popular tools (VS Code extension, GitHub Actions, npm package)
- Developer advocates or community program (give early users a platform)
- ProductHunt, Dev.to, Hashnode

**Growth:**
- Technical SEO (target "how to X" developer queries)
- Open-source strategy: core is open → enterprise features are paid
- Conference talks at relevant developer events
- Partner with tools in the same workflow (integration-led growth)

### Positioning for Dev Tools
- **Principle:** Show, don't tell. A good README with a clear example beats any marketing copy.
- Lead with the **specific problem it solves**, not the technology behind it
- Benchmark against alternatives in the README (developers will anyway)
- Make the "time to hello world" as short as possible (first experience is the pitch)

### Key Metrics
- GitHub stars / forks (social proof)
- CLI installs / API calls (activation)
- Active developers (WAD / MAD)
- Enterprise conversion rate (from free dev usage to paid team/org plan)

### Common Mistakes
- Marketing-heavy website, not enough technical depth
- Too long to first working example
- Ignoring the community (developers amplify or kill products via word of mouth)
- Hiding the pricing / being non-transparent (developers hate this)

## 6. Physical Products (D2C / E-commerce) {#physical}

### GTM Reality
Physical products have fundamentally different economics than SaaS:
- **COGS + fulfillment** eat margin — channel choice is existential
- **Returns** are costly — wrong customers cost money
- **Repeat purchase** drives LTV — first purchase is often breakeven or a loss
- **Discovery** is the core challenge: most products die unknown, not unloved

### GTM Model Selection
```
High visual / impulse product        → Social commerce (TikTok Shop, Facebook/IG)
High-consideration / premium product → D2C brand + content trust-building
Commodity / price-driven product     → Marketplace (Shopee, Lazada, Amazon)
Niche / enthusiast product           → Community + content + D2C
```

### Acquisition Channels by Stage

**Validation (0 → first 50 orders):**
- Sell to your network first — friends, family, colleagues who fit ICP
- Post in relevant Facebook groups, communities where ICP hangs out
- Marketplace (Shopee) as demand test — zero upfront cost, but low margin
- TikTok organic — if product is visual, a single viral video can validate demand in days
- Do NOT run paid ads until you have >10 organic sales and know conversion rate

**Early traction (50 → 500 orders):**
- Identify which source produced the best customers (repeat buyers, lowest return rate)
- Double down on 1–2 channels only
- Start building D2C if unit economics work (margin > 50% after fulfillment)
- UGC (User Generated Content): give product to micro-influencers for authentic content
- Build email/Zalo OA list from day 1 — owned channel, zero marginal cost

**Growth (500+ orders):**
- Paid social (Meta, TikTok Ads) — only once you know CAC from organic
- SEO if product has search intent ("best X for Y")
- Retail/distribution once you have velocity data to show buyers
- Subscription or bundle offers to increase LTV

### Unit Economics Check (before scaling)
```
Revenue per order: [price]
- COGS: [cost of goods]
- Fulfillment: [shipping + packaging]
- Returns/refunds: [% × order value]
= Gross profit per order

- CAC (by channel): [cost to acquire customer]
= Contribution margin per new customer
```
**Rule:** If contribution margin on first order is negative, you need either repeat purchase rate > 2x OR you're losing money at scale.

### Key Metrics
- Gross margin per order (target >50% for D2C viability)
- CAC by channel
- Repeat purchase rate (target >30% at 6 months for FMCG/consumables)
- Return rate (monitor by product and by channel — high return rate = wrong ICP or misleading product page)
- LTV:CAC (target >3:1)

### Common Mistakes
- Running ads before knowing conversion rate on product page
- Selling on every channel at once → spread too thin
- Optimizing for revenue instead of profit
- Ignoring return rate until it's a cash flow problem
- Under-pricing to compete on Shopee → margin collapse

## 7. Healthcare Products (Supplement / Medical Device / Wellness) {#healthcare}

### GTM Reality
Healthcare products operate under unique constraints:
1. **Trust is the primary purchase barrier** — efficacy and safety claims must be credible
2. **Regulatory risk** — claim violations can get products delisted or fined
3. **B2B2C is often more powerful** — doctors/pharmacists recommending > direct advertising
4. **Repeat purchase is the business model** — most products only work if used consistently

### GTM Model Selection
```
OTC supplement / wellness product   → B2C content trust + community + KOL/pharmacist
Medical device / clinical product   → B2B2C (HCP channel) + clinical evidence
Hospital / clinic product           → Pure B2B institutional sales
Subscription wellness               → D2C brand + education-first content
```

### The Healthcare GTM Hierarchy
Start with the highest-trust channel, then build outward:

```
Tier 1: Word of mouth from satisfied users (most credible, zero cost)
Tier 2: HCP recommendation — pharmacist, doctor, specialist
Tier 3: Credentialed KOL (medical professional with audience)
Tier 4: Community endorsement (patient groups, parent groups, wellness communities)
Tier 5: Content marketing (SEO, YouTube — educate before selling)
Tier 6: Paid acquisition (only after trust foundation exists)
```

### Acquisition Channels by Stage

**Validation (0 → first 100 customers):**
- Sell directly to people you know who have the health problem
- Seed product with 5–10 pharmacists / doctors who can provide honest feedback
- Build initial UGC: document real user journeys with consent
- Do NOT run ads yet — invest in trust assets first

**Early traction:**
- Pharmacist seeding program: provide free stock for pharmacists to recommend + earn margin
- Facebook/Zalo groups for the target condition (new moms, diabetes support groups, etc.)
- Health content: YouTube or TikTok answering questions about the condition (not the product)
- Micro-KOL (local doctor, pharmacist with 5K–50K followers) — more trusted than celebrities

**Growth:**
- Build clinical backing: fund a small pilot study, get published or get institutional testimonial
- Expand pharmacy / clinic distribution
- SEO for symptom/condition searches ("how to treat X", "best supplement for Y")
- Performance marketing only after organic conversion rate is proven

### Claim Strategy (Regulatory Safe)
| Instead of... | Say... |
|---------------|--------|
| "Cures insomnia" | "Supports healthy sleep patterns" |
| "Clinically proven to reduce cholesterol" | "Contains ingredients associated with healthy cholesterol levels" |
| "FDA approved" (if not) | "Manufactured in GMP-certified facility" |
| "Doctors recommend this" | "Formulated with guidance from clinical nutritionists" |

**Always:** Let customer testimonials carry the strong transformation claims — user-generated claims are lower regulatory risk than brand claims.

### Key Metrics
- HCP recommendation rate (% of pharmacists/doctors who actively recommend)
- Repeat purchase rate (30/60/90 day — critical for supplements)
- NPS and "would you recommend to family/friend" (healthcare purchases are highly social)
- Return/complaint rate (a proxy for product quality and ICP fit)
- Channel margin contribution (pharmacy distribution takes 30–40% margin)

### Common Mistakes
- Making strong efficacy claims without clinical backing
- Relying on celebrity KOL instead of credentialed health professionals
- Ignoring pharmacist channel (most trusted OTC recommender in Vietnam/SEA)
- Competing on price for premium health products (undermines trust)
- Running social ads before having social proof assets (UGC, testimonials, clinical claims)

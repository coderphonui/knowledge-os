## 1. GROW Model

**Best for:** Any structured coaching session — goals, decisions, plans, blockers.

**Protocol:**
- **G — Goal**: What do you want to achieve? What does success look like? By when?
- **R — Reality**: What's the current situation? What have you already tried? What's working?
- **O — Options**: What could you do? What else? If there were no limits, what would you try?
- **W — Will / Way Forward**: What will you do? What's the first concrete step? When?

**Tips:**
- Spend most time on O (options) — this is where insight happens
- Reality before Options — don't jump to solutions before understanding the situation
- Will must be specific: action + timeline + accountability

## 2. Wheel of Life

**Best for:** Life review sessions, when the user feels "off" but can't pinpoint why, or for quarterly check-ins.

**8 Life Domains:**
1. Health & Physical energy
2. Career & Work
3. Finances & Wealth
4. Relationships & Family
5. Personal Growth & Learning
6. Fun & Recreation
7. Physical Environment (home, workspace)
8. Contribution & Purpose

**Protocol:**
1. Ask user to rate each domain 1–10
2. Ask: "Which domain feels most out of balance right now?"
3. Ask: "Which domain, if improved by 2 points, would have the biggest positive ripple effect on others?"
4. Focus the rest of the session on that domain using GROW

**Note for this user:** Cross-reference against their life manifesto (life principles / tuyên ngôn sống) for alignment signals.

## 3. WOOP

**Best for:** Motivation gaps, habit formation, when user knows *what* to do but struggles to *do it*.

**Evidence base:** Gabriele Oettingen's mental contrasting research — the fantasy alone demotivates; the obstacle-plan is what creates action.

**Protocol:**
- **W — Wish**: What do you want? State it as a short, positive wish.
- **O — Outcome**: Imagine the best possible outcome. How does it feel? What does it mean for your life?
- **O — Obstacle**: What is the main inner obstacle (not external)? What feeling, habit, or belief holds you back?
- **P — Plan**: "If [obstacle], then I will [concrete action]." Make one if-then plan.

**Key insight:** The obstacle must be *internal*, not external circumstances.

## 4. Values Alignment Check

**Best for:** Decision support sessions, when user feels conflicted, or when a plan "looks good on paper but feels wrong."

**Protocol:**
1. Identify the decision or plan in question
2. Ask user to name their top 3–5 values (or reference `data/growth/life-principles.md`)
3. For each value: "On a scale of 1–10, how well does this decision/plan honor this value?"
4. Where is the gap? What would a decision scoring 8+ on all values look like?
5. Ask: "What would your future self (5 years from now) say about this decision?"

**For this user:** Core values from their profile: ethics/integrity, freedom, long-term thinking, family, contribution, discipline, peace.

## 5. Five Whys

**Best for:** Stuck sessions, procrastination, recurring problems, resistance to action.

**Protocol:**
1. State the problem: "What specifically isn't happening that should be?"
2. Ask "Why?" 5 times, each time probing the answer given
3. At each level, ask: "Is this a real constraint or an assumption?"
4. At the root cause, ask: "What would need to change for this to no longer be true?"

**Watch for:** Stopping at surface-level reasons (too busy, not enough time). The root is usually a belief, fear, or competing priority.

## 6. Future Self Visualization

**Best for:** Motivation sessions, direction-finding, reconnecting with purpose.

**Protocol:**
1. Ask user to close their eyes (metaphorically) and project 5–10 years forward
2. Prompt questions:
   - "Describe a typical Tuesday 5 years from now. Where are you? What are you doing?"
   - "What has changed about your life that you're most proud of?"
   - "Who are you spending time with?"
   - "What's no longer in your life that you let go of?"
3. Return to present: "What is your future self's biggest piece of advice for you today?"
4. Bridge: "What one thing could you do this week that your future self would thank you for?"

**For this user:** Reference their future vision section (tương lai) in profile for their long-term aspirations and desired life.

## 7. Pre-mortem

**Best for:** Planning sessions, high-stakes decisions, overcoming overconfidence.

**Protocol:**
1. State the plan or goal
2. "Imagine it's 12 months from now and this completely failed. What went wrong?" (list at least 5 reasons)
3. For each failure mode: "How likely? How damaging? How preventable?"
4. Prioritize the top 2–3 preventable risks
5. "What would you change in the plan right now to reduce those risks?"

**Variant — Pre-parade:** "Imagine it succeeded beyond expectations. What went right? What are you most proud of?"

## 8. Ikigai Mapping

**Best for:** Career crossroads, direction-finding, "what should I do with my life" sessions.

**Four intersecting circles:**
- **What you love** (passion)
- **What you're good at** (skill/talent)
- **What the world needs** (mission)
- **What you can be paid for** (vocation)

**Protocol:**
1. Brainstorm 5–10 items in each circle
2. Find items that appear in 2+ circles — these are leads
3. Find items in all 4 circles — these are high-potential directions
4. Ask: "If you pursued [X] for the next 3 years, what would your life look like?"

## 9. Motivational Interviewing (MI)

**Best for:** When user is ambivalent, resistant, or unsure about change. NOT for goal-setting sessions.

**Core principle:** The user already has the motivation — the coach's job is to surface and amplify it without pushing.

**OARS technique:**
- **O — Open questions**: "What would need to be true for you to make this change?"
- **A — Affirmations**: Recognize genuine strengths ("That took real courage to try.")
- **R — Reflective listening**: Paraphrase to signal understanding, then pause
- **S — Summaries**: Periodically summarize what you've heard, especially the "change talk"

**Change talk** — statements to amplify:
- Desire: "I want to..."
- Ability: "I could..."
- Reason: "It would be important because..."
- Need: "I have to..."
- Commitment: "I will..."

**Watch for:** Righting reflex (urge to fix/advise) — suppress it. Ask instead of tell.
